#### Tomato blight window opens — wet late July is peak risk
`Heat: Medium` · `Momentum: RISING` · `Tier 1 — Confirmed` · `Category: Gardening`

- **What:** First blight symptoms typically appear mid-to-late July in zone 6. Early blight shows as dark concentric-ring spots on lower leaves; spores spread by overhead water and rain-splash from bare soil. Today's heavy rain (¼–½") plus warm temps is ideal infection weather.
- **Why it matters (for you):** Act this week: strip the bottom 12–15" of foliage, mulch to block soil splash, and switch to drip/base watering. Resistant varieties for future plantings: 'Mountain Magic', 'Defiant PhR', 'Plum Regal', 'Bella Rosa'.
- **Source:** UConn IPM / Penn State Extension — https://ipm.cahnr.uconn.edu/tomatoes-are-you-ready-for-late-blight/

#### Spotted lanternfly adults emerging (July molt)
`Heat: Medium` · `Momentum: SUSTAINED` · `Tier 1 — Confirmed` · `Category: Pests`

- **What:** Through July, SLF fourth-instar nymphs (red/black with white spots) complete their final molt into winged adults. Feeding pressure rises fast on tree-of-heaven, grapevines, and fruit plants; PA remains under quarantine.
- **Why it matters (for you):** Scout grapevines and any tree-of-heaven now; destroy adults/nymphs on sight and check for egg-laying later in the season. July is the most effective control month.
- **Source:** PA Dept of Agriculture / Penn State Extension — https://extension.psu.edu/spotted-lanternfly-management-guide
